# **You’re Using AI for Slides Wrong: How to Build a Real Presentation Stack in 2025**

If you believe the marketing pages, we’ve reached paradise.

**_“Upload your PDF, type three words, go make coffee. When you come back, there’s a perfect pitch deck, on-brand, with charts, icons, and a bonus TED-talk script.”_**

In reality? You get eleven slides of beige, a pie chart that lies, and a stock photo of two men in suits shaking hands over a _fax machine_.

Let’s talk about how to actually use AI to build presentations, brand visuals and stories without losing your dignity — or your audience.

You’re choosing a **stack**.

## 1\. The “just upload the doc” fantasy is dead. Use a pipeline.

Every presentation tool now has the same shiny button:

> “Turn this document into slides.”

It sounds perfect. It works like a student forced сдавать курсовую: technically, yes, but you’ll hate yourself when you see it.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*ySCHP2oi9vGd0Fg9xQP95Q.png)

**What actually works is boring and effective:**

1. **Outline first, slides later.** Use a text model (Gemini, or your local LLM if you think Google is stealing your genius) to turn your idea into a proper outline:

- section structure
- clear slide titles
- concise bullet points

**2\. Then feed that into slide generators.** Don’t ask them to “understand your 20-page doc”. Give them **clean, pre-chewed bullet points**. Suddenly quality jumps from “high school group project” to “junior analyst who tried”.

**3\. Keep control.** No matter what tool you use, assume you’ll:

- move sections
- rewrite a couple of slides
- fix at least one truly stupid chart

**_AI is excellent at getting you from 0 → 0.7._**

**_The last 0.3 is on you. And that’s the part your audience remembers._**

**_Now — which tools actually deserve to be in this pipeline?_**

## 2\. Gamma, Manus, Genspark, Chronicle: the main slide engines

I ran the usual suspects through this pipeline with the same content: a simple deck about AI agents.

**First: ChatGPT/Gemini or another LLM does the text**. Then I feed that into **the generators.**

### Gamma: the “default answer” for sane people

[Gamma](https://gamma.app/) is the one I recommend to anyone who doesn’t want drama.

- Handles raw text well.
- Has different processing modes, including **“leave as-is”** and **“shorten”** — and shortening actually _cuts_, it doesn’t rewrite everything in LinkedIn tone.
- Templates are modern, varied, and not allergic to whitespace.
- You can easily regenerate or extend sections while editing.
- There’s even an agent for **bulk edits**: “make the whole deck more visual”, “tone down the cringe”, etc.
- Limits are generous and it exports nicely.

**_Gamma won’t make you a design god, but it will make you look like someone who has seen a deck before. That’s already above average._**

### Manus: HTML nerd with a data viz kink

[Manus](https://manus.im/) does something clever: presentations are basically **HTML** under the hood.

**Why that matters:**

- Tables and data visualizations look more like real dashboards, less like “PowerPoint 2003 called”.
- Layout is more flexible, especially for complex sections.
- If it messes up the layout (it will), you hit **Smart Optimize** — essentially _regenerate layout but don’t murder my content_.

Manus also exports to Google Slides, though the export can rough up the layout. The good news: its own editor is perfectly usable.

**_If your deck includes numbers, charts, or anything beyond three bullet points and a meme — Manus deserves a spot in the stack._**

### Genspark: painfully slow, annoyingly good

[Genspark](https://www.genspark.ai/) is the tool that makes you wait… and then you can’t even get mad.

- It took ~9 minutes to build a 10-slide deck.
- But the result looked like it came from a **junior consultant**:
- structured, reasonably on-brand, and in fluent Russian when asked.
- Excellent with data visualization and structure.
- Has its own editor and can export to Slides.

If Gamma is “default sane”, and Manus is “data-friendly”, **Genspark is:**

> “I’m presenting to someone important and don’t want to die.”

**_You pay with time. But you get quality._**

### Chronicle: minimalism done right

[Chronicle](https://chr.so/) is not trying to be everything. It’s trying to be **beautiful**.

- Few templates.
- But they are extremely clean and tasteful.
- No automatic images. No clever infographics.
- It’s basically: **text, layout, typography**.

For **minimalist decks, docs, or talks where content matters more than fireworks**, Chronicle is perfect.

**_Think: strategy memo, manifesto, internal doc that you don’t want to look like an HR announcement in 2011._**



## 3\. Tools to watch: Snapdeck, Figma Make, Napkin, Slidesorator, one-click toys

These ones are not “main stack” yet, but worth keeping an eye on.

### Snapdeck: chaotic good diagrams

[Snapdeck](https://www.snapdeck.app/) is weird and interesting.

- Great at **schemes, diagrams, tables**.
- You can build visual stories, not just title + text + stock photo.
- But spacing and layout are still messy. You’ll be nudging elements for a while.
- Free tokens disappear quickly (I burned through mine in ~12 slides).

**_It feels like a visual thinker’s toy. If they fix the rough edges, it could be very good._**

### Figma Make: not ready, but you can see the future

[Figma Make](https://www.figma.com/make) should have been a monster: Figma + AI + presentations.

**Right now:**

- You edit by **prompt only**.
- Free tokens run out almost immediately.
- Export to PDF/PPTX is broken or missing.
- Sometimes the output is just… buggy HTML.

**_This is still a lab experiment, not a tool. But you can smell where it’s going: AI that lives inside your design system, not next to it._**

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*_V9X6xPUHjWs3y-sH7optw.png)

### Napkin: the missing brain for Chronicle

[Napkin](https://app.napkin.ai/) is not a slide generator at all. It’s a **concept visualizer**.

- It builds networks of ideas from your notes.
- Perfect for seeing structure, clusters, surprising relationships.

**_In a just world, Napkin would plug straight into Chronicle and auto-generate ultra-clean conceptual slides._**

**_In our world, you screenshot Napkin and drop it into Gamma._**

### Slidesorator: PDF → talk in two minutes

[Slidesorator](https://www.slidesorator.com/) does one thing: **converts PDFs into slide decks**.

- It can add an **AI avatar** to present for you.
- Interface is simple enough for students and teachers.
- Supports Russian and plenty of other languages.
- Has a free tier, no card required.

**_Is it subtle? No._**

**_Is it better than “teacher reading tiny 10-pt text from a PDF”? Absolutely._**

### Allweone & getalai: the “I have 3 minutes” options

[Allweone](https://presentation.allweone.com/) and [getalai](https://getalai.com/) are in the same family:

> “Type a topic, get a presentable deck.”

**They:**

- Pick a design.
- Structure slides.
- Let you edit palette, order, elements.

They won’t impress McKinsey, but they will rescue you when someone pings at 16:35:

> “Can you present this at 17:00?”

## 4\. NotebookLM, Gemini, Dynamic View: Google’s half-finished revenge arc

Google has decided that if it can’t own _all_ of your life, it will at least own your learning materials.

### NotebookLM: from research dump to deck (and video)

[NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/) was **already good for:**

- turning a pile of research into structured notes
- answering questions grounded in your sources

**Now it can:**

- turn those notes into **presentations and infographics**, powered by **Nano Banana Pro**
- pick layouts and visuals
- build a coherent narrative through your material

You upload your text, **then prompt something like:**

> “Present this pitch deck like a founder to early-stage investors.”

It doesn’t just explain content; it **performs** it: structure, pacing, speaking points.

**_For teaching, internal trainings, or explainer decks, this is enormous._**

I’ve quietly shifted from Gamma to NotebookLM for a lot of my own decks, **simply because:**

- my research is _already there_
- it now emits slides and videos that are “good enough” with almost no friction

### Gemini’s slide mode: fast, mid, and very Google

I asked Gemini to:

> “Make a slide presentation about the best AI agents on the market.”

**The results:**

~13 slides in ~2 minutes — very fast.

- It **now**:
- auto-generates infographics
- exports to Google Slides
- Content is… fine. Facts are shallow but not wrong.
- Design looks like “corporate template #7” and that’s being generous.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*H6waEUG-EanPBztdXF6nlQ.png)

**I then compared it head-to-head with:**

- [Manus](https://manus.im/)
- [Genspark](https://www.genspark.ai/)
- [Runable](https://runable.com/)
- — an “AI agent platform” with slide mode

**On the exact same prompt**

- **Gemini**: fast, mid-content, mid-design. Think: average high school student.
- **Manus**: 10 slides in ~4.5 minutes. Content and design noticeably better, though export to Slides needs cleanup. Think: second-year university student.
- **Genspark**: 10 slides in ~9 minutes. By far the best structure, language, and visuals. Think: junior consultant at a serious firm.
- **Runable**: not ready for public consumption. Think: “PowerPoint 2007 tutorial result”.

So is Gemini competitive as an AI slide generator?

Short answer: **not with the specialists**.

### Dynamic View: tiny feature, actually useful

Gemini also quietly shipped **Dynamic View** in the UI:

- You ask a question.
- Gemini generates an **interactive explanation**: diagrams, toggles, visual steps.
- You can share it with learners or colleagues.

It’s a small feature but surprisingly impactful for education and internal training.

Think of it as “auto-whiteboard mode for one topic”.

## 5\. Beyond slides: charts, brand, fonts, and story tools

A good deck is not just slides. **It’s:**

- solid visuals for your data
- coherent brand
- tolerable typography
- and occasionally, an actual story

**_AI has quietly invaded all of these layers._**

### Plotset: finally, charts that don’t look like accounting software

[Plotset](https://plotset.com/) is what happens when someone gets tired of ugly dashboards.

- **300+ templates** for charts and data stories.
- Full edit control, export to JPG/PNG/SVG.
- **Built-in AI assistant:**“I have this dataset, show me X vs Y”, “make this interactive”, “improve this layout”.

**Perfect companion for Gamma / Manus / Genspark:** build your visuals in Plotset, drop them into your deck, look like you know what “data storytelling” means.

### Pomelli: AI creative director for your brand

[Pomelli](https://labs.google.com/pomelli/) is one of the rare cases where Google shipped something that feels like a **product**, not a demo.

You give it your website URL.

**It scrapes:**

- copy
- visuals
- style and tone

**Then it generates:**

- product shots
- social posts
- ad creatives

And — crucially — lets you **edit font, color palette, and copy** after generation.

**It’s basically a vertical wrapper around Nano Banana Pro:** a brand-aware, editable Canva-killer prototype.

**_Is it perfect? Of course not._**

**_But as a way to rapidly prototype campaigns that actually match your brand instead of random Dribbble, it’s genuinely useful._**

### Canva, Affinity, video, and the font circus

If you’re anywhere near design or marketing, your life already touched **Canva**.

**A few new bits worth noting:** [Canva’s video editor](https://www.canva.com/video-editor/)

**lets you:**

- edit videos in a familiar UI
- and now **generate clips by prompt**

Canva is giving away **Affinity** for free: [Affinity Studio](https://www.affinity.studio/get-affinity)

- full-fat vector, photo, and layout apps for macOS & Windows
- essentially a gift to anyone building brand assets

**Canva Pro lets you:**

- upload **custom fonts**
- define brand kits with colors and logos
- keep your decks and posts on-brand without thinking too hard

Now combine that with the **font tools** ecosystem:

1. **drFonts** ( [drfonts.com](https://drfonts.com/)) — promises: “upload a logo or visual, get a custom font in seconds” nice idea, suspicious execution:

- AI is still pretty bad at high-quality vector outputs
- site smells a bit like “internet marketer made this on a Sunday”
- free trial that requires a paid plan first, bad reviews on X

**2\. WhatFont** Chrome extension ( [link](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/whatfont/jabopobgcpjmedljpbcaablpmlmfcogm)) — hover text on any site, get:

- font family
- weight,
- size,
- line height,
- color

**3\. WhatTheFont** ( [whatfonttool.com](https://whatfonttool.com/)) — for fonts in images and screenshots.

**4\. Fontjoy** ( [example link](https://t.me/NeuralProfit/920)) — AI that suggests font pairings that don’t look like ransom notes.

**5\. Deblank** — color palette + brand preview tool for your site, merch, app.

**6\. Photo Gradient** ( [photogradient.com](https://photogradient.com/)) — generates gradients in your brand colors, with a Figma plugin.

**7\. Window Resizer** ( [Chrome extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/window-resizer/kkelicaakdanhinjdeammmilcgefonfh)) — check how your layouts look across devices.

Add image generators like Midjourney, Flux, Ideogram ( [ideogram.ai](https://ideogram.ai/t/my-images)), Recraft, Microsoft Designer — and suddenly you’re not “making a deck”.

You’re running a **mini creative studio** where AI handles the dull bits.

### Visual Story-Writing & Chandra: storyboards and source extraction

If you write stories, game narratives or complex plots:

**Visual Story-Writing** by Damien Masson

- GitHub: [https://github.com/m-damien/VisualStoryWriting](https://github.com/m-damien/VisualStoryWriting)
- Demo: [https://damienmasson.com/VisualStoryWriting/](https://damienmasson.com/VisualStoryWriting/)
- Hooks into OpenAI (GPT-4o) via your API key

**Maintains:**

- a **map** of your world
- a **graph** of your characters
- a **timeline** of events
- You move things in the map/graph → it updates the text.
- You change the chronology → it rewrites the story.

It’s like having a story engine where the visuals and text are genuinely linked.

**For raw source material:**

- **Chandra OCR** from Datalab

**Handles:**

**Perfect for:**

- scanning old PDFs

## 6\. So what’s the actual stack?

Let’s put it all together. In 2025+ you are not “using AI to generate a presentation”.

You’re assembling a **presentation stack** that might look like this:

1. **Input / extraction**

- PDFs, images → [Slidesorator](https://www.slidesorator.com/)/ [Chandra OCR](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra)
- Existing research → [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/)

**2\. Thinking & structure**

- Outline + bullets → Gemini / your favorite LLM
- Concept maps → [Napkin](https://app.napkin.ai/)
- Interactive explainers → Gemini **Dynamic View**

**3\. Data visuals**

- Charts → [Plotset](https://plotset.com/)
- Diagrams → [Snapdeck](https://www.snapdeck.app/)

**4\. Deck generation**

- General purpose → [Gamma](https://gamma.app/)
- Data-heavy / HTML-based → [Manus](https://manus.im/)
- “Make me look elite” → [Genspark](https://www.genspark.ai/)
- Minimalist text → [Chronicle](https://chr.so/)
- Emergency, 3 minutes left → [Allweone](https://presentation.allweone.com/)/ [getalai](https://getalai.com/)

**5\. Brand & visuals**

- Brand-aware creatives → [Pomelli](https://labs.google.com/pomelli/)
- Logos → [Squarespace Logo](https://www.squarespace.com/logo/)/ Recraft / Midjourney
- Fonts → [drFonts](https://drfonts.com/)(try carefully), [WhatFont](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/whatfont/jabopobgcpjmedljpbcaablpmlmfcogm), [WhatTheFont](https://whatfonttool.com/), Fontjoy
- Colors & gradients → Deblank, [Photo Gradient](https://photogradient.com/)
- Video → [Canva video editor](https://www.canva.com/video-editor/), NotebookLM video mode

**6\. Output / teaching**

- Decks + talking points → NotebookLM
- Interactive explanations → Gemini Dynamic View
- Video lessons → NotebookLM, Canva

**_Pick 2–3 from each stage and you have a setup that would’ve required a small agency five years ago._**

## 7\. The one rule that actually matters

It’s tempting to ask:

> “Okay, but what is the **best** AI presentation tool?”

**_Wrong question._**

The real question is:

> **“Where do I still want to think?”**

**Because AI will happily:**

- invent numbers to fill your chart
- make fake logos
- choose fonts that look like a 2009 wedding invitation
- generate eleven near-identical slides that say nothing

**Your job is to decide:**

- which **parts** of the process can be automated
- and where **human judgement** still matters

For most people, a sane trade-off looks like this:

**Let AI:**

- chew through messy input
- propose structure
- generate first visuals
- keep things consistent with your brand

**But you must:**

- set the story
- decide what stays, what goes
- check the numbers
- ensure the deck doesn’t insult your audience’s intelligence

**AI will absolutely get better.**

Two years ago, we were laughing at hands with seven fingers. Now people are complaining their **AI-generated infographic** isn’t historically accurate for a specific year.

**_The same will happen with decks._**

**_But we’re not there yet._**

Right now, if you want **fast, good-enough, not embarrassing** decks, the game isn’t:

> “Which model is smarter?”

**It’s:**

> “Can you build a stack where each tool does one thing well — and you stay awake at the wheel?”

**_Do that, and you’re already ahead of 90% of the “just upload the doc” crowd._**
